Nodus retrieves around twenty papers for a research question, extracts up to twelve claims from each, and clusters equivalent claims into a cited report. 02 — The problem

A summary of forty papers takes a minute. Defending it takes a week.

Existing tools answer the easy question. You get a fluent paragraph with a row of citations under it, and then a colleague asks which paper a specific sentence came from, and you are back in the PDFs.

Three questions decide whether a finding survives review: which paper is this actually from, why do these two disagree, and how much should I trust it. Nodus is built to answer those three, in a form you can check line by line.

03 — Three axes

Lineage

Every cluster carries the chronological chain of papers behind it: the originating paper and its year, the span of years the claim survived, and how each later paper relates to it.

Relationships are typed — origin, supports, contradicts, extends — so you can read the shape of the evidence, not just its size.

Disagreement

That two papers conflict is easy to notice. Nodus states why. Each cluster gets typed disagreement drivers across eight categories, and every driver names the specific papers involved.

methodology population metric definition temporal sample size analysis publication bias other
methodology Cooney 2013 ↔ Noetel 2024

One review restricts to trials with blinded outcome assessment and finds a small effect; the other pools unblinded trials in a network model and finds a moderate one.

population Blumenthal 1999 ↔ Schuch 2018

Clinically diagnosed older adults in a supervised trial against a general adult cohort self-reporting activity levels.

metric definition Babyak 2007 ↔ Cooney 2013

Remission rate on HAM-D versus standardised mean difference in continuous symptom scores — the same trials, two effect measures.

Quality, computed — not judged by a model

A tier comes from a published formula: study design 40%, sample size 20%, corroboration 20%, extraction confidence 20%, minus a conflict penalty of up to 15%. No language model rates the evidence.

Every input is exposed, so the arithmetic is auditable. Change your mind about it and you override the tier by hand — the computed value stays on the record beside yours.

04 — How it works

Three stages, and it takes minutes

A run streams its progress phase by phase because it is doing real work on real papers rather than autocompleting a summary. Expect minutes, not seconds.

Stage 1

Retrieve and rank

Around twenty papers come from Semantic Scholar, ranked by a stated composite rather than an opaque relevance score.

normalized citations40%
influential citations30%
recency20%
relevance10%

Stage 2

Extract and cluster claims

Up to twelve claims per paper are extracted, embedded, and grouped with equivalent claims from other papers. The cluster keeps each source claim, its stance, and its extraction confidence.

20 papers→ up to 240 claims
claims→ clusters
cluster→ lineage + drivers + tier

Stage 3

Analyse and synthesise

Sections assemble as they finish, each citing the clusters behind it. Follow-up questions are scoped to a previous query and linked to their parent, so the refinement chain stays inspectable.

Export → PDF · Markdown · JSON · HTML
The PDF is the print variant of the document on screen, so it cannot drift from it.

05 — Human in the loop

Your correction is recorded beside the computation, not on top of it

You know things the formula does not: that a trial's registry entry was amended, that a cohort double-counts. Override the tier and give a reason. Both values stay visible.

Edited clusters and reports are pinned. Re-analyse the question and your work survives instead of being overwritten by the next run.

06 — Deployment and privacy

Runs on your hardware, with your model

Nodus is self-hosted. Papers, claims, clusters and reports live in your own Postgres — there is no hosted service holding them.

The model is swappable: Azure OpenAI, Anthropic, or Ollama. With Ollama the whole pipeline runs locally and nothing leaves the machine, which is the configuration to use for unpublished or embargoed work.

08 — FAQ

Where do the papers come from?

Semantic Scholar. Retrieval, citation counts and influential-citation counts all come from its API.

Can it run offline?

The pipeline can, via Ollama — extraction, clustering and synthesis all run locally. Retrieval needs Semantic Scholar, so papers already in your database can be re-analysed offline while new queries cannot.

What does it not do?

It does not judge quality with a language model — tiers are arithmetic. It does not replace reading the paper: it tells you which papers to read and what to check when you do.

How long does a run take?

Minutes. Twenty papers, up to twelve claims each, embedding and clustering, then synthesis — progress streams phase by phase while it works.
